Each phylum is described by its typical adult body plan, simplified: sponges are shown without symmetry (a few are radial), echinoderms are scored by their five-part adults (their larvae are bilateral), arthropod body cavities are counted as a reduced coelom, and chordate segmentation (muscle blocks) is accepted either way. The tree is the current DNA-based working hypothesis with some branches collapsed. Animation speed is exaggerated.

    Part 2: Embryo Viewer

    Embryo comparison
    FeatureProtostomeDeuterostome

    Typical patterns, simplified. Many protostomes (such as snails and annelids) show spiral, determinate cleavage and schizocoely; arthropods and some others differ. Deuterostomes show radial, indeterminate cleavage and enterocoely, though vertebrates form their coelom in a modified way. Hours of development are compressed into seconds.
