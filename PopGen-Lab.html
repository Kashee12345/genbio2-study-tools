Model: a Wright-Fisher population of N diploid individuals with non-overlapping generations. Each generation, selection (relative fitness = 1 − s for each genotype), then one-way migration from a source population, then mutation from A to a, then random sampling of 2N alleles to form the next generation (genetic drift). Mating is random. Mutation rates are set far higher than real rates (about 10−5) so their effect is visible. Each panel shows up to 60 individuals; when N is larger, the 60 dots are a sample in the right proportions.

  • Drift only: compare N = 10 with N = 1,000. How fast do replicates spread out and fix?
  • Set s for aa to 0.5. Does selection or drift win when N = 10? When N = 500?
  • Heterozygote advantage: s for AA = 0.12, Aa = 0, aa = 0.8. Where do the lines settle?
  • Press Bottleneck in a large population with p = 0.1. How often is the rare allele lost?

Part 2: Hardy-Weinberg Trainer

Problems are randomly generated and assume Hardy-Weinberg conditions unless the question tests them. Frequencies can be typed as a decimal (0.04), a percent (4%), a fraction (1/25) or "1 in 25". Answers within about 2% of the exact value are accepted, so rounding is fine. Chi-square answers are accepted within about 0.05 or 2%.
